Rain garden math

A rain garden goes wrong in three places: the downpour that was never converted from centimeters on the forecast into liters off the roof, the bed dug to a size somebody half-remembered instead of a share of the drainage area, and the mulch bought by the armload instead of by the bed. Rain garden math turns the roof into the liters, turns the roof into the bed, and turns the bed into the bags. Exact arithmetic, every borrowed number labeled.

Open the calculators

The three tools

  • Catch plan - the roof area and the rain at a labeled runoff coefficient become the liters looking for somewhere to go.
  • Bed plan - the roof area at a labeled percent rule becomes the square meters of bed to dig.
  • Mulch plan - the bed at a labeled depth becomes the liters and the bags of mulch.

Labeled norms

Labeled norms: a 0.9 runoff coefficient for hard surfaces, a 15 percent bed-to-roof sizing rule, 8 cm of mulch and 50 L bags - all illustrative defaults you can change by measuring. Real sizing depends on your soil's infiltration rate, so run a labeled bucket perk test before you dig, and give the water a safe overflow path that never points at a foundation, a neighbor or a septic field. This is planning arithmetic, not a drainage design.
